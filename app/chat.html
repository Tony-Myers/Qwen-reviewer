why, and what to avoid asking
Explanations are generated and have been reliable here. Particular facts have to be recalled, and this has no way to look anything up, so the questions to avoid are the ones that require a fact rather than an account:
  • Which paper or study established X? Who proposed X? Every such question in testing produced a defective or invented reference.
  • What did study Y find? Same, and the invention is harder to spot because the finding is usually about right.
  • What is the threshold, the cut-off, the number? The rule for how many imputations to use came back inverted, twice, in opposite directions.
Where a term means different things in different fields, say which you mean: asked about the E-value it described a Bayesian measure of evidence rather than the sensitivity measure used in observational research, confidently and with an invented source.

Across eleven independent test questions it gave mostly sound explanations alongside invented papers carrying real-looking DOIs, a four-author paper assembled from the initials of a method, a verbatim quotation that does not exist in the book it was attributed to, and the wrong statistic under the right name. Nothing from here belongs in a review unchecked.
